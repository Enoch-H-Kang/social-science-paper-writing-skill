# Literature and Positioning Guide (Method Papers)

## Goal

Place the paper among the substantive and methodological literatures its question needs. Four or five streams, or literature distributed through the paper, may be appropriate. State gaps only where the cited work and paper's question warrant them.

## Workflow

1. List the relevant literatures and closest papers; let the question determine the number of streams.
2. Use paragraphs or distributed discussions of the length each comparison needs.
3. State the paper's position where the comparison becomes informative; a final combination sentence is optional.

## Paragraph Template (per literature)

**Source and variation.** The structural exemplar groups four streams, while the ML-method exemplar distributes related work across sections. Precise closest-work distinctions recur; the four paragraph slots are editorial aids rather than a required common layout.

1. Topic sentence naming the literature by its question.
2. What the cited work established, in enough detail to represent it fairly.
3. Its precise difference, limit, or foundation for this paper, when relevant.
4. Transition to the paper's question where useful; a gap is not compulsory for every stream.

## Rules

1. **Position against questions and actual papers.** Paper-by-paper comparisons are useful when accurate and fair.
2. **The closest paper gets the most respectful and precise treatment.** State its contribution in terms its author would accept, then the one axis of difference, as a question rather than a resource.
3. **Cite the canonical version.**
4. **Make citation groups legible.** Several citations can establish a taxonomy when their common role is explained.
5. **Cite method literatures for capability and failure modes.** Cite both the source of an estimator or algorithm and the paper that characterizes when it fails in settings like yours.
6. **Theory papers:** the positioning move is "existing models generate [pattern A] but cannot generate [the dichotomy we document]." The gap is a phenomenon the model must produce, stated before the model appears.
7. **Fence the novelty (ML-method papers).** Add a labeled Discussion paragraph naming the one or two closest algorithms or frameworks and the precise axis of difference.
8. **Structural variant.** Explain a relevant structural difference when it matters to the question; no stream needs a compulsory dismissal.

## Checklist

1. Are the relevant literatures represented, including four or five streams or distributed discussion where warranted?
2. Closest work treated precisely and respectfully, with the difference stated as a question?
3. Every claim about prior findings traceable to the cited paper's result?
4. Is the paper's position clear, whether stated at the end or distributed through the discussion?
5. Is the track-specific rule applied (phenomenon gap for theory, structural axis for structural, fenced novelty for ML)?
