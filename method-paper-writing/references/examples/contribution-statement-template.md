# Contribution Statement Template (Method Papers)

Audience-aware contribution options for the introduction. Lead with the paper's headline contribution, which can be a finding. Bracketed content is invented.

**Source and variation.** Methodological, substantive, and managerial audiences recur in the structural and ML-method exemplars, but their lead contribution differs. Separate paragraphs, the three-slot format, and any actor rule are editorial adaptations.

## Skeleton

1. **Methodological.** `We provide a [model / framework / algorithm] that makes it possible to [capability]. [Optional sourced contrast: Compared with [closest work], this addresses [specific question].]` State a capability, not a technique name.
2. **Substantive.** `We show that [phenomenon] arises because [mechanism], which [reframes / settles] [question]. [Optional sourced contrast.]`
3. **Managerial / policy.** `For [actor], our results imply [supported guideline or consequence, with a number only if relevant]. [Optional sourced contrast.]`

## Optional two-part statement

When useful, connect the technical answer to its business perspective. An actor rule is optional and needs direct support. The bid-shading/waiting formulation sometimes used as an illustration is an invented, unproved placeholder and must not be presented as a corpus result.

## Rules

1. A finding may be a contribution when the statement explains why it matters to the field.
2. When contrasting closest work, cite a source and describe the precise difference respectfully, as a capability or question.
3. Order by the paper's headline, then the other relevant audiences.

## Checklist

1. Is the methodological paragraph a capability rather than a technique name?
2. Is any closest-work contrast precise, respectful, and supported by a source?
3. If an actor rule appears, is it supported and useful?
4. Does any repeated finding explain its significance as a contribution?
