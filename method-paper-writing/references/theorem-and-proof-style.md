# Theorem Statements and Proofs — Theory Track

Use this guide for analytical papers whose central results are characterizations, equivalences, existence results, or comparative statics, and for writing their supporting proofs. It supplements the existing theory arc. Choose a statement and proof structure that serves the contribution and the intended audience; the source papers do not share one compulsory sequence.

## Prepare the statement

Announce the economic question or claim before developing the machinery needed to state it. Immediately before a result, explain its job, define the required objects, or finish the derivation it summarizes. There is no fixed runway length.

Separate maintained primitives from result-specific restrictions. Check what is fixed, what is universal, what is existential, and what the constructed object may depend on. Conditions may live in a clearly invoked definition, but the reader must be able to recover the scope. Keep any scope restriction central to the contribution visible near the statement.

State one logical relation where possible. Use numbered parts for distinct restrictions or cases. Leave motivation, proof strategy, interpretation, and literature comparison outside the formal block. A short theorem may depend on nearby definitions; it need not repeat the whole model.

## Build the result sequence

Explain which result answers the main question and which results supply its construction or technical support. A definition → characterization → equivalence route can replace a candidate-equilibrium or benchmark route when that is the paper’s question. Do not require every paper to use definitions, lemmas, propositions, theorems, and corollaries in a fixed ladder. Name results descriptively when it helps retrieval.

Place interpretation close enough to explain what the result restricts, what remains possible, why it holds, or which special case it includes. Interpretation may follow a short proof. Comparative statics and counterintuition are optional and require support. Use examples to explain an object, demonstrate attainment, or show the consequence of relaxing an assumption.

## Choose the proof location

Keep a proof or derivation in the body when it explains the central construction. Defer technical verification or supplementary calculations when that improves the main argument. Identify whether a pointer leads to a proof, example, calculation, or established source result. A survey may explicitly summarize an established theorem; a new research claim needs a complete formal statement and support.

## Write the proof

Open with the specific obligation: fix an arbitrary object, invoke a prior result, reduce to a remaining inclusion, or identify the candidate to construct. For equivalences, distinguish directions. For extensions, separate the inherited argument from the new one.

Organize by statement parts, directions, cases, or construction and verification. Use local claims for technical subarguments when useful; step numbering is optional. Define auxiliary notation at first use and explicitly map any relabeling or normalization to the original objects.

Verify that a constructed object is admissible and satisfies the claimed property. For limiting arguments, check that the limit preserves the required feasibility or consistency. Cite established machinery and compress routine algebra only when the omitted task and its justification are identifiable. Keep the new result’s essential construction, incentive or feasibility checks, consequential boundary cases, and quantifier argument explicit.

Close by connecting the last step to the assertion: the required inequality, contradiction, inclusion, or attainment. Use a consistent end marker without adding a compulsory interpretation paragraph to every proof.

## Organize the appendix

Refer deferred proofs back to stable result identifiers. State a result in the appendix if it was only announced informally in the body. Organize calculations by the body sections they support when appropriate. Make reordered dependencies findable; neither restating every theorem nor restarting result numbering is mandatory.

Retain enough explanatory prose to show why a proof step works. Supplementary examples may need fuller economic interpretation and figures. Do not treat the appendix as equations only.

## Review

Check scope and quantifiers; the role of each supporting result; explanation near the headline; the accuracy of proof pointers; construction plus verification; justified omissions; declared notation changes; and an explicit logical endpoint. This checklist is an editorial aid, not a required outline for every proof.
