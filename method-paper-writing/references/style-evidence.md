# Style Evidence Base (Method Track)

This file records the published papers the rules in this skill were distilled from, so a user or a future editor can check a rule against its source. The patterns are paraphrases of structure and rhetoric, not reproductions of the papers' text. Quoted phrases are kept to a few words.

Venues: *Management Science*, *Marketing Science*, *Journal of Marketing Research*, *Information Systems Research*, *Quantitative Marketing and Economics*.

## The Corpus (method track)

| Paper | Role | Signature move to imitate |
|---|---|---|
| Cloak or Flaunt? The Fashion Dilemma (Marketing Science, 2012) | Theory exemplar | Dichotomy opening with matched real cases; mirrored tradeoff sentences; benchmark first, then the main result; comparative statics with economic names; an endogenous-emergence close. |
| The Value of Reputation in an Online Freelance Marketplace (Marketing Science, 2013) | Structural exemplar | Challenges argued with a data figure before the approach; nested inferior models kept on stage; identification in prose with a worked example; counterfactuals posed as manager questions. |
| LOLA: LLM-Assisted Online Learning Algorithm for Content Experiments (Marketing Science, 2025) | ML-method exemplar | A negative diagnosis billed as a co-contribution; limiting-case validation; gains located by traffic regime; a deployment-focused close. |
| Targeting and Privacy in Mobile Advertising (Marketing Science, 2021) | Empirical-primary hybrid, cited here for its method craft | A toy example before the general model; a shared primitive as the hinge between components; a proposition with justified simplifications. |

## Cross-Paper Patterns

1. **Diagnosis before design.** The tool is motivated by a named failure that is demonstrated: existing models cannot generate the dichotomy, naive estimators are biased, or the pure approaches fail.
2. **Intuition before formalism.** Premises are built in words, lemma and proposition intuitions sit in the text, proofs are deferred, and every formula gets a plain reading.
3. **Enumerated architecture.** Components, propositions, challenges, and validation steps are numbered, and the enumeration matches the section structure.
4. **Track-specific validation.** Theory: benchmark first, with both regimes generated. Structural: identification in words, fit against nested inferior models before counterfactuals. ML: limiting cases nest the incumbents, ablations confirm the intuition, and gains are located by regime.
5. **On-the-spot defense of choices,** by required properties, by tractability, or by a business constraint.
6. **Actor-facing close:** a manager question answered, deployment conditions, or a one-sentence emergence result a reader can repeat.

## How to use this file

When you edit a rule in this skill, check it against the published versions of the cited papers. Do not quote from them beyond short phrases. Add a paper to the table only after the pattern it shows has been paraphrased into a rule elsewhere in the skill.

## Checklist

1. Is each rule you changed still consistent with at least one cited paper?
2. Are all descriptions here paraphrases, with no quoted phrase longer than a few words?
3. Does every table row give title, journal, and year, and nothing else about authorship?
4. Does every pattern listed here appear as a rule somewhere in the skill?
