# Style Evidence Base (Empirical Track)

This file records the source papers the rules in this skill were distilled from, so a user or a future editor can check a rule against its source. The patterns are paraphrases of structure and rhetoric, not reproductions of the papers' text.

Sampled venues: *Management Science* and *Marketing Science*. Other journals named in the entrypoint are target venues, not sources of this evidence.

## The Corpus (empirical track)

| Paper | Role | Signature move to imitate |
|---|---|---|
| Design and Evaluation of Optimal Free Trials (Management Science, 2023) | Empirical exemplar | A prevalent practice opens the abstract; the decision question needs no actor conflict. The abstract ends on a scoped negative comparison supporting a caution, while the conclusion ends on a concrete generalizability question. |
| Targeting and Privacy in Mobile Advertising (Marketing Science, 2021) | Empirical-primary hybrid | Market importance opens the abstract without a numeric scale claim; the introduction later develops actor conflict. An explicitly constructed example illustrates the model, and the abstract ends on a hedged incentive implication. |
| The Value of Reputation in an Online Freelance Marketplace (Marketing Science, 2013) | Method-primary hybrid, cited here for its empirical craft | Descriptive evidence is treated as suggestive before rival-model failures are measured; counterfactuals are conditional on the specified demand-side framework. |

## Cross-Paper Patterns

1. **Opening sentence is a market fact in plain words.** Scale may be qualitative; neither empirical exemplar opens with a method or citation.
2. **The question follows the practice:** opposing effects or an open decision can suffice. Conflicting incentives belong where the setting actually has them.
3. **"To answer these questions, we…" is the hinge** of the abstract. The framework is an answer to questions, never a contribution in search of a problem.
4. **Frameworks have enumerated components,** and the enumeration in the abstract matches the section structure.
5. **Quantitative comparisons are baselined and substantively consistent:** a percentage gain needs a recoverable comparator; compatible rounding is acceptable, and an abstract need not contain numeric market scale.
6. **Heterogeneity is a finding.** Segments can explain outcomes or policy assignments; the policy rule belongs only where evaluation supports it.
7. **Closes vary:** a scoped negative comparison can support a caution, while a hedged incentive implication can close another abstract. Decisive conditions attach to the claims they bound.
8. **The findings preview follows the paper's question:** the field experiment revisits segmentation and mechanism after policy value, while the targeting hybrid moves from prediction and targeting value to a model and market counterfactuals. Independent policy evaluation supports learned-policy claims when made.

## How to use this file

When you edit a rule in this skill, check it against the listed source version. Do not quote from it beyond short phrases. Add a paper to the table only after the pattern it shows has been paraphrased into a rule elsewhere in the skill.

## Checklist

1. Is each rule you changed still consistent with at least one cited paper?
2. Are all descriptions here paraphrases, with no quoted phrase longer than a few words?
3. Does every table row give title, journal, and year, and nothing else about authorship?
4. Does every pattern listed here appear as a rule somewhere in the skill?
