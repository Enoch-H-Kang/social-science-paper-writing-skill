# Style Evidence Base (Empirical Track)

This file records the published papers the rules in this skill were distilled from, so a user or a future editor can check a rule against its source. The patterns are paraphrases of structure and rhetoric, not reproductions of the papers' text. Quoted phrases are kept to a few words.

Venues: *Management Science*, *Marketing Science*, *Journal of Marketing Research*, *Information Systems Research*, *Quantitative Marketing and Economics*.

## The Corpus (empirical track)

| Paper | Role | Signature move to imitate |
|---|---|---|
| Design and Evaluation of Optimal Free Trials (Management Science, 2023) | Empirical exemplar | The full arc in one abstract: a surprising average effect, then a mechanism scorecard, then an off-policy-evaluated personalized policy and its gain against the named uniform policy, ending on a caution. |
| Targeting and Privacy in Mobile Advertising (Marketing Science, 2021) | Empirical-primary hybrid | Introduction split into context / agenda and challenges / approach; question sets and challenges mapped onto a two-component framework; an incentive flip at the close. |
| The Value of Reputation in an Online Freelance Marketplace (Marketing Science, 2013) | Method-primary hybrid, cited here for its empirical craft | Descriptive evidence before structure; the bias of naive estimators quantified rather than asserted; counterfactuals posed as manager questions. |

## Cross-Paper Patterns

1. **Opening sentence is a market fact in plain words,** with scale. No paper in the empirical corpus opens with a method, a citation, or a "despite recent advances" clause.
2. **The tension arrives by sentence two or three:** a real disagreement among firms, or between industry and regulators.
3. **"To answer these questions, we…" is the hinge** of the abstract. The framework is an answer to questions, never a contribution in search of a problem.
4. **Frameworks have enumerated components,** and the enumeration in the abstract matches the section structure.
5. **Numbers are exact and baselined in the abstract:** a percentage, a named status quo or best uniform alternative.
6. **Heterogeneity is a finding.** Segments are defined by their optimal treatment and then interpreted, so the reader can say who should get what.
7. **The last sentence changes what someone will do** (a regulator, a manager, a platform), or it states the caution.
8. **Results follow one fixed order:** average effect, mechanism, heterogeneity, policy value, with train and test side by side and the test authoritative.

## How to use this file

When you edit a rule in this skill, check it against the published versions of the cited papers. Do not quote from them beyond short phrases. Add a paper to the table only after the pattern it shows has been paraphrased into a rule elsewhere in the skill.

## Checklist

1. Is each rule you changed still consistent with at least one cited paper?
2. Are all descriptions here paraphrases, with no quoted phrase longer than a few words?
3. Does every table row give title, journal, and year, and nothing else about authorship?
4. Does every pattern listed here appear as a rule somewhere in the skill?
