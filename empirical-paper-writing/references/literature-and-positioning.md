# Literature and Positioning Guide (Empirical Papers)

The literature may be separate or distributed through the introduction and later sections. Its job is positioning the question.

## Goal

Place the paper among the substantive and methodological literatures its question needs. Four or five streams, or a distributed discussion, may be appropriate. State the paper's question or difference at the point where it helps the reader.

## Workflow

1. List the relevant literatures and their questions; a privacy/targeting, personalization, and auction grouping is one editorial adaptation.
2. Use as much space as the closest work and substantive distinctions need, whether in one section or distributed.
3. Explain the paper's position where the comparison becomes informative; a final combination sentence is optional.

## Finding citations

1. **Mark, don't invent.** When a contextual fact, method capability or failure mode, closest-work contrast, or established piece of machinery needs support, mark the claim as needing a citation instead of writing a reference from memory.
2. **Search for candidates.** Check publisher pages, scholarly indexes, and the literature citing the closest work.
3. **Verify before use.** Use a candidate only after seeing a real record whose title, authors, venue, and year match; prefer the canonical journal version over a working paper.
4. **Attach to the exact claim.** The citation must support the sentence it sits in; support for a neighboring claim does not count.
5. **Leave visible gaps.** If no verified source is found, leave `[CITATION NEEDED: <the claim>]` in the draft and list it in the report to the user. Never fill the gap with a plausible-sounding reference.
6. **Keep a running list.** Record each found citation with the exact claim it supports.

## Paragraph Template (per literature)

**Source and variation.** Separate substantive and methodological streams recur across the empirical exemplars; one uses five streams and another four. The sample opener below reflects the targeting hybrid, while the paragraph slots and a distributed-literature alternative are editorial adaptations.

1. Topic sentence naming the literature by its question: "A first stream of work asks how much targeting information is worth to advertisers."
2. What the cited work established, in enough detail to represent the closest work fairly.
3. A precise difference, limit, or connection when relevant; some streams establish a foundation without a gap.
4. Transition to the paper's question where it helps the argument.

## Rules

1. **Position against questions and actual papers.** Concise paper-by-paper comparisons are useful when they accurately establish the closest distinction.
2. **The closest paper gets the most respectful and most precise treatment.** State its contribution in terms its author would accept, then the one dimension on which this paper differs, and make the difference a question ("they estimate the value of targeting under the observed regime; we ask what happens to each player's payoff under counterfactual regimes"), not a resource ("we have more data").
3. **Cite the canonical version.** Journal version over working paper when one exists.
4. **Make citation groups legible.** Multiple citations may establish a taxonomy; explain why the group belongs together.
5. **Cite method capabilities and limits when relevant.** A failure mode may come from the paper's own comparisons rather than a required paired citation.
6. **Connect streams to the question where useful;** no per-stream gap sentence is required.
7. **One stream may be cautionary.** Prior work showing the naive version of your own approach failing (personalization that does not beat a uniform rule, say) pre-answers the methods reviewer and motivates your design.

## Checklist

1. Are the relevant streams represented, including four or five streams or distributed literature where the paper needs them?
2. Closest work treated precisely and respectfully, with the difference stated as a question?
3. Every claim about prior findings traceable to the cited paper's actual result?
4. Is the paper's position clear, whether stated at the end or distributed through the discussion?
5. Is one stream, where relevant, used cautionary to pre-answer the methods reviewer?
